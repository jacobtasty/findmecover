Banging, gurgling or whistling boiler

Boilers aren't silent, but a new or louder noise is usually a sign of something specific. Here's what each one tends to mean.

Written by Jacob, founder of FindMeCover

Carbon monoxide warning. If your carbon monoxide alarm goes off, or you notice a lazy yellow or orange flame, soot or staining around the boiler, or feel headaches, dizziness or nausea that ease when you're away from home: turn the boiler off, open windows, get outside and call the National Gas Emergency Service on 0800 111 999.

Banging, or a kettle-like rumble

Often called kettling. It's usually caused by limescale or sludge building up in the heat exchanger, which makes water overheat and boil in places. It can also come from low pressure or a pump problem. Kettling won't fix itself and makes the boiler work harder, so get an engineer to look at it.

Gurgling

  • Air in the system. Bleed your radiators, then check the pressure.
  • Low pressure. Check and top it up.
  • A frozen condensate pipe, if it's freezing outside. How to thaw it.

Whistling

Often the same cause as kettling: limescale or sludge restricting the flow. It can also be low pressure. If topping up the pressure doesn't stop it, call an engineer.

Humming or vibrating

Can come from the pump running too fast or a loose part. A pipe vibrating against a wall or floor is often harmless, but an engineer should check the boiler itself.

Clicking without lighting

If the boiler clicks repeatedly but doesn't fire up, it may be struggling to light. That's often down to the gas supply or a frozen condensate pipe. See our guides to ignition fault codes on the boiler help page.
