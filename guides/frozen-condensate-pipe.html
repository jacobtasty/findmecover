How to thaw a frozen condensate pipe

If your boiler stops working on a freezing morning, this is the first thing to check. It's often a ten-minute fix with a kettle.

What is the condensate pipe?

Modern condensing boilers produce waste water as they run. It drains away through the condensate pipe, a plastic pipe, usually white or grey, that runs from the boiler to a drain. When part of it is outside, it can freeze in a cold snap. The water backs up and the boiler shuts down.

Signs it's frozen

  • It has been below freezing, often for a day or more.
  • The boiler shows a fault code and won't relight. Common ones are Worcester Bosch EA 227, Vaillant F28, Ideal L2 and Baxi E133.
  • You can hear gurgling from the boiler or the pipe.

How to thaw it

  1. Find the pipe. Follow it from the bottom of the boiler to where it goes outside. The frozen section is usually the coldest, most exposed part: a bend, a dip or the open end over the drain.
  2. Pour warm water along it. Use a watering can or jug of warm water. Not boiling: very hot water can crack the pipe. A hot water bottle or a warm cloth held against the pipe also works.
  3. Keep going until it runs freely. It may take a few goes.
  4. Reset the boiler as your user manual describes. It may take a few minutes to start working again.

Stay safe. Only thaw pipes you can reach from the ground. Don't climb a ladder in icy weather, and watch out for the water freezing on the path. If the pipe is high up, call an engineer.

Stopping it happening again

An engineer can insulate the outside section of the pipe, fit a wider pipe that's less likely to freeze, or reroute it so more of it runs inside. It's worth asking at your next service, before the next cold snap.
